#### Re-computation, arithmetic shown

Per user-day, from the proposal's own assumptions (15 min/day over 3 sessions, 20 picks updating every 5 min, one live game every 15 s, 300–400 B payloads, ~3.5 KB handshake per session):

```
pick updates      = 20 picks × (15 min ÷ 5 min)          =  60
game updates      = 15 min × 60 s ÷ 15 s                 =  60
delivered updates =                                         120
payload bytes     = 120 × 300 … 120 × 400                = 36,000 … 48,000 B
handshakes        = 3 sessions × 3,500 B                 = 10,500 B
per user-day      =                                         46,500 … 58,500 B  (46.5–58.5 KB)
per user-month    = × 30 days                            =  1.395 … 1.755 MB
```

| DAU | Peak concurrent (10%) | Downloads/month (300 B … 400 B) | Free allowance (360 MB/day × 30) | Billable | **RTDB/month** | Web PubSub units @80% (allowed set) | **Web PubSub/month** | Ratio |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 10,000 | 1,000 | 13.95 – 17.55 GB | 10.8 GB | 3.15 – 6.75 GB | **$3 – $7** (proposal $11) | 1.25 → **2** | **$98** (proposal $98) | 14–31× (proposal 8.9×) |
| 50,000 | 5,000 | 69.75 – 87.75 GB | 10.8 GB | 58.95 – 76.95 GB | **$59 – $77** (proposal $97) | 6.25 → **10** | **$489** (proposal $405) | 6.3–8.3× (proposal 4.2×) |
| 200,000 | 20,000 | 279 – 351 GB | 10.8 GB | 268.2 – 340.2 GB | **$268 – $340** (proposal $419) | 25 → **50** | **$2,447** (proposal $1,563) | 7.2–9.1× (proposal 3.7×) |

Web PubSub column = units × $1.61 × 30.4 days. "Allowed set" rounds up to the purchasable counts (1, 2, 5, 10, 20, 50, 100); at a literal 80% with fractional units the column is $61 / $306 / $1,224, and at the proposal's effective peak÷625 packing it is $98 / $392 / $1,566. Assumptions that push the RTDB number *down* and are not in the model: the monitor polls every 90 s only inside T-15m and every 5–180 min further out (A14), and live-scores writes game state every 30 s, not 15 s (B7).

**Verdict on the numbers:** the arithmetic and the 3–9× claim hold. Both columns err in Firebase's favour — the RTDB column over-states Firebase's bill by ~1.3–1.6×, and the Web PubSub column under-states Azure's by ~25% against its own stated 80% assumption and by much more once unit counts must be purchasable (E2), so the two errors partly cancel and the direction of the recommendation is unchanged either way. The dominant assumption is **peak concurrent connections** (10% of DAU) — it drives 100% of the Web PubSub column and none of the RTDB column, and because RTDB bills delivered bytes, a wrong peak estimate (e.g. NFL-Sunday clustering making peak 30% rather than 10%) triples only the Azure side. The assumption that dominates the RTDB column is **how much each client actually listens to** — the proposal's "listen only to picks on screen and detach on blur" (L261) is the cost lever, and a listener bug is the only realistic way RTDB becomes the expensive option.

## What this means for the assessment

- **The transport recommendation survives, and its flagged pricing caveat can be closed.** Every load-bearing external number in the section checks out against Microsoft's own sources: 1,000 connections/unit, the 80% guidance, 100 units Standard / 1,000 Premium P2, **$1.61 per Standard unit-day**, $1 per million messages, per-recipient broadcast billing, and the 1M-messages-per-unit-day free quota (D1–D6). The Web PubSub dollars that the proposal itself distrusted are **correct**; the third-party summary was right. No number in the section is refuted.
- **Front Door is correctly ruled out, twice over.** SSE is unsupported (D8), and the 3,000-connection / 2-hour WebSocket cap (D9) is below the 50k-DAU row's own peak, so the "BFF stream through Front Door" option was never viable — not just slow to build.
- **But the transport is not the decision that matters.** Nothing in the §"Edge Picks redesign fit" overlay rows is a transport problem: the monitor's game-pick ids can never match a card (A7/A8), `next_update_due` is never written so the contract's staleness rule cannot be implemented (A6), new picks appear once a day so mid-day publications have no live state (A9), and neither live service has code on the branch that deploys to production, with the tst deploy config still full of `TODO-` values (A4/A10/B4). Choosing Web PubSub would not fix one of these.
- **The security gap is the real cost of keeping RTDB, and the proposal states it too gently.** No RTDB rules exist in any repo — the only rules text in the whole estate is a Markdown snippet in live-scores' app guide (B5/B6). That snippet grants `.read: auth != null` at the `liveGameCards`/`liveMatches` root, i.e. every signed-in free user can read every live game; the natural way to ship `/edgePicks` is to copy it, which would expose every pick's live price and reveal which picks are locked. Spec X5 and AC-15 exist precisely because of this.
- **The gap is worse than "no rules yet": under the one documented ruleset, `/edgePicks` has no read grant at all.** Firebase rules default-deny, so as written the edge overlay is unreadable by clients and the pilot's overlay feature cannot work until someone writes a rule — at which point the entitlement question is unavoidable, not deferred. That decision is D6 (overlay owner), and it is the same decision for either transport, because the same payloads are being protected.
- **Per-field gating means a writer change, not a config change.** Because shallower rules override deeper ones and a grant at a path grants the whole subtree (C5), premium fields have to live in their own node; the proposal's split-node advice is the only correct shape. Combined with the missing opaque `overlay_key` (A15 — the key is deterministic from `league` + `{bet_id}|{region}`, and the id is already in the BFF payload), the current design gives no per-pick authorisation story at all, and the spec's AC-13/AC-15/AC-38 cannot pass.
- **Custom claims are workable but bounded by the token, not by the purchase.** Claims take effect on the next ID token, and ID tokens last an hour (C6). A lapsed subscriber keeps reading premium nodes for up to that hour unless the app forces `getIdToken(true)` on the entitlement event — the spec asks for that (7.4) and it belongs in the pilot's definition of done, not in a follow-up.
- **The overlay half is not "built", it is "written but never consumed".** The app has no `@react-native-firebase/database` (B8), the contract's overlay merge rules (7.3) have never run against a real node, and the monitor's node shape has one field the contract asks for missing (`live_edge`) and one never populated (`next_update_due`, A6/A13). Treat the row "built today" as "server-side writers exist"; the client work is unstarted for both transports.
- **Cost-wise the choice is small in absolute terms and robust in direction.** At the largest modelled row the difference is ~$1.1k–2.1k/month; at 10k DAU it is under $100/month. The 3–9× claim holds (E3), and because RTDB bills bytes while Web PubSub bills concurrency, a wrong peak estimate moves only the Azure column — so the recommendation is safe against the assumption it is most exposed to, and unsafe only against a listener-scope bug on the RTDB side.
- **One number the proposal should fix before it is quoted.** The Web PubSub column is priced at an effective ~62.5% unit utilisation while the table says 80%, and its implied unit counts (8 and 32) are not purchasable Standard/P1 counts; the nearest allowed counts make it $489 and $2,447 (E2). Correcting it strengthens the recommendation rather than weakening it, and it removes the obvious "your own arithmetic doesn't use your own assumption" line of attack.
- **Two monitor defects should be fixed regardless of the transport decision, and both are cheap.** The precision divergence at the contract's own E6 boundary — the monitor returns `live` where the spec requires `narrowing`, because it compares unrounded floats (A13) — is a contract-test failure waiting to happen, and `unknown` can never be emitted by the writer. Both are small, test-visible changes in `shared/edge_status.py`.
- **The recommendation's own revisit trigger is the right one.** Web PubSub becomes attractive when overlays are per-user (open positions, My Picks P&L), because that fan-out is exactly what its per-recipient message billing and group model are for (D6). Until then, the pilot's real blockers are (1) D6 — write per-pick read rules and stop shipping predictable keys, (2) the id/`next_update_due`/cadence defects, and (3) a tst path with a named RTDB owner. If those are done behind an app-side `OverlaySource` interface (L259), the transport can be swapped later at the cost the proposal claims.
